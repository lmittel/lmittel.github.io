z-scores

The z-score of a value x is z = (x - \mu)/\sigma, the number of standard deviations x lies above or below the mean. If X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), then (X - \mu)/\sigma \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1). The heights below are of US women and men, each group modeled as a Gaussian.

IV

Where does π come from?

The kernel e^{-x^2/2} has no closed form antiderivative, so the area A under it cannot be found directly. The two-dimensional Gaussian looks harder but is easier, and A can be recovered from its volume.

drag to turn

tubes

A is the area under the kernel, A = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2/2}\,dx. Every slice of f is that kernel times a constant, so A shows up in its area. The slice areas add up to 1, and that pins down A.
V

Round and square: only the Gaussian

Round means rotationally invariant, so f depends only on x^2 + y^2. Square means X and Y are independent with the same distribution, f(x, y) = g(x)\,g(y).

largest errorThe largest difference, over all s, between the exact P(S_n \le s) for the sum S_n of n components and its normal approximation \Phi((s - n\mu)/(\sigma\sqrt{n})), where \Phi is the standard normal CDF. By the Central Limit Theorem, it goes to 0 as n grows.

Each outcome \omega = (x_1, \ldots, x_n) has probability p(\omega) = p(x_1) \times \cdots \times p(x_n), so the pmf of S_n is P(S_n = k) = \sum_{\omega : S_n(\omega) = k} p(\omega).
