Density isn't probability

For a continuous univariate random variable, probability is area under the density curve: P(a < X < b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx. The height f(x) is a density: probability per unit of x. Here X \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \sigma^2). Drag a and b, or grab the shaded area and slide it.

V

Where does π come from?

The standard bivariate Gaussian density is e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}/(2\pi). Drop the constant and look at the bump e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2} = e^{-x^2/2}\,e^{-y^2/2}. Because it factors, the volume under it is \bigl(\text{area under } e^{-x^2/2}\bigr)^2. Because it is round, the same volume can be added up in rings, and the rings give exactly 2\pi.

VI

Round and square: only the Gaussian

Square means independent: the joint density is the product of its marginals, f(x, y) = f_X(x)\,f_Y(y). Round means rotation invariant: f depends only on x^2 + y^2. Drag the dot to find a density that is both.
